The house

A planter’s bungalow that kept its slow hours.

Built by a Scottish tea planter in 1889 and lived in by the same estate family since 1947, Kanchen House sits on a spur of the Upper Dhotrey garden, 640 acres of tea falling away toward the Rangeet valley. We restored it board by board: teak floors, Burma-teak verandahs, the original cast-iron fireplaces, and the long windows that frame the third-highest mountain on earth.

There are fourteen rooms, never more than twenty-eight guests, and a staff of thirty-one who mostly grew up on this hill.

Experiences

Days that follow the tea.

  1. Basket on your back, learn the “two leaves and a bud” pinch from pluckers who do it eleven thousand times a day.

  2. Six garden teas, cupped and slurped the professional way, in the 1902 factory tasting room. Leave with a 100 g caddy of your favourite.

  3. Our driver knows the quiet ridge below the tower. Blankets, a hot flask and parathas packed by the kitchen.

  4. The UNESCO-listed Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, under steam, round the Batasia loop. We book the first-class seats.

  5. Butter lamps at Yiga Choeling, momos in a Ghum kitchen, and the drums of the Peace Pagoda at dusk.

  6. River stones from the Rangeet, warmed oils of wild ginger and timur pepper. Direct guests receive 10% off every treatment.

Before you come

Good questions.

Anything else, write to stay@kanchenhouse.in or WhatsApp the front desk on +91 354 225 0189. We reply within the hour, 7 am to 11 pm.

When can we actually see Kangchenjunga?

Most reliably from mid-October to mid-December and again in March–April, usually from sunrise until mid-morning. In the monsoon (June–September) it hides for days at a time, though the cloud forests are at their most beautiful. Our verandah forecast above is updated hourly.

What does booking direct include that OTAs don’t?

Our lowest rate (we’ll match any lower public rate and take a further 10% off), late checkout until 2 pm, a welcome tea tasting, 10% off spa treatments, and a free Bagdogra or NJP transfer for stays of four nights or more. You also deal with us directly if plans change.

What is your cancellation policy?

Free cancellation up to 14 days before arrival on flexible rates. Within 14 days we retain one night’s charge. Peak dates (20 Dec – 5 Jan, Durga Puja week) require 30 days’ notice.

Is the house suitable for children and older guests?

Children of all ages are welcome; the Tea Master’s Cottage is ideal for families. The estate is hilly: the Garden Rooms are step-free from the drive, and we have a buggy for the steeper paths.

Is there heating, Wi-Fi and power backup?

Every room has a working fireplace or wood stove plus oil-filled heaters and electric blankets. Fibre Wi-Fi throughout, and full generator backup. Mobile signal is good on Jio and Airtel.

Do Indian and foreign guests need permits?

No permit is needed for Darjeeling. Foreign nationals visiting Sikkim need an Inner Line Permit, which we can arrange in advance.

Letters from the estate

Harvest notes, the house’s quiet weeks before anyone else hears, and the occasional recipe from the kitchen. No more, we promise.
